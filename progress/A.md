**2) Devpost: "How we trained it"**
- **Data:** 1,773 Nemotron-generated multi-turn conversations (6 scam families plus benign, 275+61+56 hard negatives across splits), each labeled turn by turn with a stage and the strike turn. 27 near-copies (cosine > 0.95) were dropped before splitting. Split by conversation 70/15/15: 1,245 train, 269 val, 259 test.
- **Leakage defenses:** splits are by conversation (never by message) and by generator call, so siblings from one Nemotron prompt can never straddle train and test. Temperature, alert threshold, lock-on threshold and early stopping use VAL only. The held-out zero-day family is removed from base training and VAL. An independent re-implementation cross-checks the early-warning and lead-time metrics. A leakage review found and fixed one split leak right before submission, and the numbers above are the post-fix ones.
- **Architecture:** a small causal sequence model. MiniLM turn embeddings go through a GRU and CSTMD1-style attention into three heads: scam family, next stage and turns-to-strike. It reads one turn at a time, so it can warn before the strike. A lock-on rule stops the alert from flickering on and off.
- **Calibration:** temperature scaling fit on VAL; ECE falls from 0.052 to 0.024. The alert threshold is set so benign conversations false-alarm at most 1% on VAL. The risk shown in the UI is 1 − P(benign).
- **Zero-day:** a new scam is described in one sentence, Nemotron writes synthetic conversations, and a tiny adapter head (a "chloroplast") trains on top of the frozen model in about 0.2 s on CPU. No customer text is used. We hold out `job_task` to test this. The real run is pending, so we show `--` rather than the proxy number.
- **Honest limits:** (1) We have **no human-written test set yet** (`test_human` is empty), so the **cross-generator gap is unmeasured**: every number is on Nemotron-style text and probably flatters us. PreyNet catches 38% of scams from the first message alone, a sign it may lean on generator style. (2) The 1% cap is tuned on only 133 benign VAL conversations (one false alarm), and TEST FPR is 0.77%, so treat it as about 1%, not exact. (3) Spread comes from 3 training seeds on one split, with no confidence interval over test conversations.
